Field Service : Rotterdam Site Notes

Created by Sofie de Vries, last modified on Mar 02, 2026

Site notes for Rotterdam Distribution Hub (Maasvlakte Fresh Logistics). Migrated from the Kyltek Kennisbank in 2024 and kept up to date since. If you're going to the roof, read the work at height section even if you've been there before. The rules changed in 2025.

Getting there

The hub is on the Maasvlakte, at the far end of the port. From Rotterdam it's 40 to 50 minutes without traffic, and there is always traffic on the A15 at the Botlek bridge between 7 and 9. Leave early or come after 9:30.

Gate: truck entrance on the north side, contractors use the separate lane on the right. Show your Corvane ID and say who you're visiting. First visit: you need the site induction (30 minutes, in the reception building, in English or Dutch), after that a visitor badge each time. They keep a list, so make sure your name is on it before you go. Pieter's team can add you.

Parking: visitor parking in front of reception. Vans can go round to the technical area behind hall 3 if you need tools close, but only if Pieter has said so that day.

Roof access and work at height

No roof work without a work at height permit (werkvergunning werken op hoogte), every visit, no exceptions. Pieter's team issues it at the technical service desk. It's valid for one day. Since 2025 the site also needs to know how many people are on the roof, so sign in and out on the roof board at the hatch.

  • Access is via the internal stair in hall 2 to the roof hatch, then a fixed ladder. Keys from the technical service desk.
  • There are guardrails along the east edge, where the chillers are. The west and north edges have no guardrails. Stay on the walkway. If you have to go off the walkway, you need fall-arrest equipment and a second person, and the permit has to say so.
  • Wind: no roof work at wind force 6 or more. The Maasvlakte is windy, check before you drive out, not when you get there. The site has a wind meter on the reception building, ask.
  • Tools go up in the bag on the hoist, not in your hands on the ladder.
  • In winter the walkway gets slippery with frost in the morning. Wait for it to clear or salt it (bucket at the hatch).

RTM-H-0007 history

0007 is the unit that keeps us busy. It's an NK-600 from 2023, and its circuit 2 has had low suction pressure alarms more than once, always after a period of very low load, usually the first big restart after the winter. Circuit 1 has been fine.

In February 2026 circuit 2 went to L-03 again after the winter restart. I inspected it myself: no leak found visually, no oil marks, the charge deficit was small, and I topped it up following KT-WI-14 with a full leak test planned for the next visit. It ran fine after that. The details are in the case and in the Servio export for the older history.

If 0007 alarms again, don't just top it up again. A unit that keeps needing charge has a reason, and we need to find it. Call me first.

Practical: on 0007, the circuit 2 service valves are on the side facing the roof edge, so you're working close to the guardrail. Kneel, don't lean. The controller is on the hatch side.

Other notes

  • The condenser coils on the roof collect salt and fine sand from the west wind, and in June the poplar fluff from the trees along the access road. Clean them in May before the warm weather, it saves a call-out in July.
  • The customer runs the cold rooms year round, but the chillers on the roof only carry the process cooling and the offices, so in winter they're nearly idle.
  • Lunch: canteen in the reception building, cheap and decent, card only.
