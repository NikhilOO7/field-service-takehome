1. Top-ups on units with sticking EXVs

What happened: GDN-H-0014 (NK-600, Gdansk) had L-03 on circuit 2 in April 2025 and again in August 2025. Both times the technician followed KT-WI-14: no leak found by visual inspection, small deficit, topped up, leak test planned for the next visit. Both times it came back. A second NK-600 at another EU customer had the same pattern over the summer.

In September 2025 Nordkyl published SB-2025-014 on the EXV-2 valve sticking after idle periods. Both serials were in the range. When Sofie replaced the valves under the campaign in October, the old valve on GDN-H-0014 was visibly sticking. The charge had probably been fine all along. We'd added refrigerant to a circuit that didn't need it, twice, and had to recover the extra afterwards.

What we take from it: a low suction pressure alarm with no leak found is not a low charge until proven otherwise. A sticking valve, an iced evaporator or low water flow give the same alarm. The serial check against open bulletins has to come first, before anyone connects a bottle.

Done: Sofie added a warning panel about the campaign to the EU Chiller Field Guide in October. Work orders for campaign work carry the campaign code so Nordkyl pays.

Not done: the instruction itself (KT-WI-14) still doesn't mention bulletins. See 2.

2. KT-WI-14 needs review

KT-WI-14 is the Kyltek work instruction for low suction pressure on chillers, from 2021, translated in 2024. It allows a top-up without a full leak test when no leak is visible and the deficit is small. Corvane's SOP-020 says never top up without a leak test. They can't both be right for the same unit, and at the moment the EU follows KT-WI-14 and NA follows SOP-020.

FS-STD-001 says the legacy Kyltek work instructions stay valid until superseded, but there's no list of which ones that covers, and nobody owns superseding them. Sofie wrote KT-WI-14 originally and is happy to rework it, but it needs someone to decide whether we're aligning on the SOP-020 rule for the EU, and that has consequences for call-out times in Gdansk while we're short of certified people (see 5).

Rachel will take it to the Q1 ops meeting. Owner TBD until then. KT-WI-14 needs review.

Until the review is done: EU technicians should call Sofie or Lena before topping up any chiller under KT-WI-14. This is a team agreement, not a procedure change.

3. Bulletins don't reach the technician

Bulletins land in an inbox, someone saves them to SharePoint, and that's where they stay. Nobody standing in front of a unit goes looking for them unless they already suspect something. The NK-600 one is the obvious example. Kenji also flagged the new Vastra firmware bulletin (SB-2026-003, out this month) and will add a note to the compressor playbook.

Actions: when a new bulletin lands, the team lead for that family posts a two-line summary in the team channel with the affected assets from the fleet register. Firmware version goes in every compressor case.

4. EU history before 2025

Since January 2025 the EU is on Salesforce, but everything before that is in Servio, and the only way to see it is the monthly export with Dutch headers. On site, nobody opens it. On GDN-H-0014 the April 2025 visit was already in Salesforce, but the earlier history wasn't visible, and that history would have shown that this unit had alarmed after idle periods before.

Rachel has asked IT. No date yet.

5. F-gas coverage in Gdansk

Only a few EU technicians hold the F-gas certificate and most of them are in Rotterdam. In Gdansk that means a non-certified technician often arrives first, can't touch the circuit, and the job waits for someone to drive out. Jakub's exam is booked for later in the year. In the meantime we pair people where we can.

The detailed numbers by region are in the quarterly ops review deck, so I'm not copying them here.

7. TS-2 replaced twice on TAC-C-0142 before the firmware link was found

TAC-C-0142 (VRS-110, Tacoma, firmware 5.0) tripped E-14 in March 2026. The controller's TS-2 reading was well above a handheld at the discharge flange, so the sensor was replaced, which was correct as far as it went. In July it did exactly the same thing: E-14, TS-2 reading high against the handheld, cooler cleaned, tripped again, sensor replaced a second time.

Only on the second call did Kenji connect it to the firmware. Vastra's SB-2026-003 explains it: on VRS-110 firmware below 5.1, a failing TS-2 is reported as E-14 (high discharge temperature) instead of E-15 (sensor fault). So on 5.0 the code alone can't tell you whether the discharge temperature was really high or the sensor was failing, and everyone treats it as a temperature problem first. From 5.1 the two are split. TAC-C-0143 next to it is on 5.1 and hasn't had any of this.

The bulletin was out since January and it was in Kenji's playbook since February. Nobody connected it at the unit. That's lesson 3 again, with a cost: two sensors, two visits, and a customer who noticed.

Action: firmware 5.1 on every VRS-110 below 5.1. In our fleet that's TAC-C-0142 only (RTM-C-0120 and TAC-C-0143 are on 5.1, GDN-C-0131 on 5.2). It isn't done yet: the update needs the X10 cable and a two-hour window from the customer, and neither has lined up. Kenji is chasing. IN PROGRESS

Kenji also thinks the second sensor failure on the same unit has a local cause, not only a firmware one, and he's following that up with the Tacoma team.
