NK-600 EXV campaign (added 14 October 2025). Nordkyl issued Service Bulletin SB-2025-014 in September 2025. On NK-600 units in the bulletin's serial range, the EXV-2 expansion valve can stick after idle periods. The unit then gives L-03 (low suction pressure) with a normal refrigerant charge. It looks exactly like a leak or an undercharge.

Before you follow KT-WI-14 or add any refrigerant to an NK-600, check the serial against the table in SB-2025-014. If it's in the range, the bulletin comes first: replace the EXV on both circuits with the rev 3 assembly (63-1180) and put the campaign code NK-C25-14 on the work order so Nordkyl pays for it. The old rev 2 valve is 63-1170, if you see one on the shelf it's for out-of-range units only.

What this covers

Nordkyl NK-300 and NK-600 air-cooled chillers at our EU sites, Rotterdam (Maasvlakte Fresh Logistics) and Gdansk (Baltic Prime Foods). All of them run R-513A. This is field knowledge from the Kyltek years, not a procedure. The procedures are on SharePoint, and where this page and a procedure disagree, follow the procedure and tell me.

Not covered: the customers' own ammonia plants (not ours, don't touch), the NA chiller at Fresno (Grace looks after that one and has her own notes), and anything to do with the chilled water side beyond the flow switch.

Before you go: certification

Anything that touches the refrigerant circuit needs an F-gas certificate (Category I). That means connecting gauges, recovering, charging, brazing, opening a valve to the circuit. Without the certificate you can still do a lot: read pressures and temperatures on the controller, look for oil marks and icing, check the condenser coil and the fans, read the alarm history, and talk to the customer. Do all that, write it in the case, and raise the work order with refrigerant certification required. The supervisor sends someone certified.

If you're working towards your certificate, come out with me or another certified engineer as often as you can. You learn more on a roof in Maasvlakte in February than on the course.

How the NK-600 is laid out

Two independent refrigerant circuits, each with its own compressors, condenser coil section, EXV-2 electronic expansion valve and filter drier. The controller shows each circuit separately, so always note which circuit alarmed. Circuit 1 is the one nearest the control panel on every NK-600 we have.

The NK-300 is a smaller single-circuit version with the same controller and the same alarm codes, so most of this page applies to both.

The controller keeps an alarm history with the suction and discharge pressure at the moment of each alarm. Read it before anything else. It tells you whether the alarm came at start-up, after an idle period, or under full load, and those are three different stories.

The alarms we actually see

Code What it means What it usually is for us
L-01 High discharge pressure Dirty condenser coil. In Rotterdam: salt, sand, and in June the poplar fluff from the trees along the access road. In Gdansk: flour dust
L-03 Low suction pressure (below 2.4 bar for 60 s) Low charge from a leak, an iced evaporator, low water flow, a blocked filter drier, or on campaign-range NK-600s a sticking EXV. See below
L-06 Chilled water flow switch Customer's pump stopped or a strainer on their side blocked. Ask before you open anything

There are more codes in the Nordkyl manual (chapter on alarms). These three are 90% of our call-outs.

L-01 high discharge pressure

Clean the condenser coil. Every L-01 I've seen in Rotterdam in summer was the coil. Use the coil cleaner and low-pressure water, from the inside out where the fans allow it. Never use a pressure washer on the fins, they fold flat and then you've made it worse permanently. Check all the condenser fans run. A dead fan on one section shows up as L-01 on that circuit only on hot days.

If the coil is clean and the fans run and L-01 continues, it can be non-condensables in the circuit after a repair that wasn't evacuated properly. That's certified work.

L-03 low suction pressure

This is the important one, and the one where we have made mistakes. The procedure for low suction pressure on chillers in the EU is KT-WI-14 Low Suction Pressure on Chillers. I wrote the original in 2021 for Kyltek and it was translated in 2024 with the other work instructions. Follow it step by step.

In short, KT-WI-14 has you rule out the causes that aren't a refrigerant loss first, inspect for leaks, and then decide what to do about the charge. It has a rule for a small deficit with no visible leak, where you can top up and plan a full leak test for the next planned visit. Read the instruction for the exact condition. Don't do it from memory.

But first read the warning panel at the top of this page. On NK-600s in the SB-2025-014 serial range, check the bulletin before you go anywhere near KT-WI-14.

Corvane has its own procedure for leak testing and recharging, SOP-020, which is stricter about topping up. As far as I know KT-WI-14 is still valid for the EU, because FS-STD-001 says the legacy Kyltek instructions stay valid until they're superseded, and nobody has superseded it. Lena has asked for the two to be reviewed together. Until then, if you're unsure, call me or Lena before you charge anything.

Things to check before you think about the charge

  1. Which circuit, and when. L-03 at start-up after a long stop is different from L-03 under load.
  2. Ice on the evaporator or the suction line. An iced evaporator gives low suction pressure with a perfectly good charge.
  3. Chilled water flow. Low flow through the evaporator does the same. Check the customer's pump and the differential across the evaporator.
  4. Filter drier. Feel both ends. If the outlet is noticeably colder than the inlet, the drier is restricting. (Kyltek rule of thumb: if you can feel the difference with your hand, it's too much.) Part 63-0410 for the core.
  5. EXV. On the controller service menu, watch the valve opening percentage while the circuit runs. If it sits at one value and doesn't move with load, or it's at 100% with low suction, the valve isn't doing its job. On campaign units, see the bulletin.
  6. Sight glass. Bubbles in the liquid line sight glass with the circuit running steady suggest a low charge. Bubbles just after start-up mean nothing.
  7. Oil marks. Where refrigerant leaks, oil comes with it. Look at flare nuts, Schrader valves, the compressor connections and the brazed joints at the coil headers.

Winter restarts

Several of our units in Rotterdam run at very low load or stand idle for weeks in winter, when the customer doesn't need much cooling. The first big restart in February or March is when L-03 tends to show up. We used to put this down to the cold. Since SB-2025-014 I'm less sure that was the whole story on the NK-600s. If you get L-03 on an NK-600 after a winter idle period, that's exactly the pattern the bulletin describes.

Leak testing

For leak searching (lekzoeken) use the electronic detector, slowly, below the joints because R-513A is heavier than air. Confirm with bubble solution before you open anything. For pressure testing (afpersen) and evacuation (vacuumtrekken), follow the work instruction or SOP you're working under. I'm not repeating the pressures and vacuum levels here, they're in the documents.

Always replace the filter drier core when the circuit has been opened.

Records

Since January 2025 we record everything in Salesforce on the work order, plus the refrigerant log for every kilo recovered or charged, with the cylinder number. The F-gas regulation requires it, and inspectors do ask. The old history (2023 and earlier) is in Servio, and you can see it in the monthly Servio export. The Servio notes are in Dutch, and a lot of them say bijgevuld (topped up) with a kilo figure. If you see the same unit topped up more than once in a year in Servio, tell me.

 Old Kyltek notes on the NK-300 (from the original page, kept for history)

The NK-300s were first installed in 2022 (RTM-H-0011). Early units had the controller on firmware 6.x, which did not log the pressure at the moment of the alarm. All our NK-300s are now on 7.2. If you find an old printout in the plant room that doesn't have pressures in the alarm list, that's why.

The NK-300 has a single circuit, so an L-03 takes the whole unit down. Customers notice those faster.
